The Morning Reset

The first light your brain sees each morning sets your internal clock for the entire day. Natural sunlight within the first hour of waking tells your body it's time to be alert — and quietly schedules your sleepiness for 14–16 hours later. Most of us skip this and start the day with a screen instead.

Light + Hydration
02

The Midday Reset

Ten to twenty minutes of gentle movement — a short walk, a few stretches — keeps your energy stable through the afternoon. It prevents the mid-day crash that sends most people reaching for another coffee. A minute or two of slow, deep breathing through the nose helps your body shift from "go mode" into calm, focused clarity.

Movement + Breath
03

The Evening Reset

Your brain needs darkness to produce melatonin — the hormone that makes you sleepy. Bright overhead lights and screens after sunset trick your brain into thinking it's still daytime, suppressing that signal entirely. Dimming your environment one hour before bed is one of the most impactful things you can do for deep, restorative sleep.

Darkness + Wind-Down
