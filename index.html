The Problem

Modern life moves at a pace our biology was never designed for. The signals are still there. We just need to listen.

You live in permanent daylight.

Overhead lights, screens, and devices have quietly cut you off from the natural signals your body needs to sleep properly. Your brain thinks it's still daytime at 10pm — so it stays alert, delays tiredness, and keeps you staring at the ceiling.

This isn't a sleep disorder. It's a mismatch between your modern environment and your ancient biology — and it's entirely fixable through small daily changes that cost nothing.

"You don't have a sleep disorder. You have a light disorder."

The Morning Reset

The first light your brain sees each morning sets your internal clock for the entire day. Natural sunlight within the first hour of waking tells your body it's time to be alert — and quietly schedules your sleepiness for 14–16 hours later. Most of us skip this and start the day on a screen instead.

Light + Hydration
02

The Midday Reset

Ten to twenty minutes of gentle movement — a short walk, a few stretches — keeps your energy stable through the afternoon. It prevents the energy crash that sends most people reaching for another coffee. A minute of slow nasal breathing helps your body shift from "go mode" into calm, focused clarity.

Movement + Breath
03

The Evening Reset

Your brain needs darkness to produce melatonin — the hormone that makes you sleepy. Bright overhead lights and screens after sunset trick your brain into thinking it's still daytime. Dimming your environment one hour before bed is one of the most impactful things you can do for deep, restorative sleep.

Darkness + Wind-Down
